Method and limitations

Lights: every indicator has a Watch and a Danger threshold. Fixed thresholds are anchored to past episodes (e.g. HY spread peaked near 5.3% in 2018Q4). Percentile-based indicators use their own history's 85th/97th percentile, computed only with data available at the time (no look-ahead).

Layer stress: the average stress of the indicators in a layer. 0 = calm, 50 = right at the Watch line, 100 = everything past Danger.

Scenario scores: A Deleveraging correction = equity stress × (1 − 0.8×credit) × (1 − 0.8×funding): highest when equities panic but credit and funding are fine. B Earnings-downgrade bear = weighted AI, AI contagion, policy and growth layers, plus a bump when capex cuts are announced. C Credit event = mainly credit and AI credit, amplified by AI contagion and equity stress. D Liquidity crisis = funding markets + cross-asset (stocks/bonds/gold falling together, dollar, yen). Weights are judgment calls from our discussion, not statistically fitted; the Backtest tab shows the score each past episode would have produced, so you can check whether the scoring makes sense.

AI funding chain: three pillars — credit alarm (BBB spread, credit-equity divergence, private credit, contagion stage ≥3, Layer B credit items), monetization (Anthropic and peers vs the checklist), and capex surprise. One failing = amber; two or more = red.

Data limits: FRED only keeps about 3 years of the ICE HY/IG/BBB spreads, so long-history backtests use Moody's Baa − 10Y instead. Taiwan margin history is backfilled from the exchange a little on each run, so its backtests get more complete over time. The margin maintenance ratio is estimated as (margin shares × close) ÷ margin loans for listed stocks; it is not the same basis as brokers' account-level ratios, so read the direction more than the level.
